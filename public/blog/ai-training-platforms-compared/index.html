Several companies connect experts and skilled generalists with AI labs and businesses that need people to evaluate and improve AI systems. Mercor, micro1 and Handshake AI are three names people ask about often. This page describes how each presents itself, what to check before you apply, and how to compare any platform. It is an educational comparison, not an endorsement, and it does not list pay rates because rates change by project and are set by each company. Last reviewed: 19 November 2026. Terms change, so treat each platform's own pages as the source of truth.

New to the topic? Start with AI training jobs: what they are and how to get started.

Mercor

How it presents itself: an AI-era talent marketplace that connects domain experts with AI labs and enterprises for recruiting, interviews and human expertise used in model training and evaluation.

How you get in: public descriptions say applicants choose a domain and take an AI-conducted interview assessing expertise, problem solving and communication. Passing can qualify you for projects in that domain.

Work described publicly: tasks such as preference judgments, rubric design, writing solutions and domain-specific evaluation, mostly as contract work.

Check on the official page: eligibility by country, the contract and payment terms, and what the agreement says about intellectual property in your work and confidentiality. Start at Mercor's expert page.

micro1

How it presents itself: a data company that supplies domain experts for AI training and evaluation work, and uses an AI interviewer to screen applicants.

How you get in: its own guides describe applying through its opportunities portal, completing a skill-based interview with its AI interviewer, verifying your identity and being onboarded, after which you are matched to projects. Passing the interview does not guarantee a role.

Work described publicly: independent contractor roles such as rating outputs, writing prompts, annotating data and reviewing domain-specific results. Listings can state that there are no fixed hours and no guaranteed volume of assignments, and that contractors handle their own taxes and expenses.

Check on the official page: pay stated on the specific listing, eligible countries, the contract terms and how payment works. Apply on the company's own site rather than through third-party links. See micro1's guide to AI training jobs.

Handshake AI

How it presents itself: Handshake's help centre describes an initiative that offers highly skilled students, graduates and researchers paid, part-time, project-based opportunities to help train and evaluate large language models.

Who it is for: current students, graduates and professionals. Handshake's FAQ describes eligibility conditions that include location and work authorization, so check them carefully, especially if you are outside the United States.

Good to know: Handshake states that it does not sponsor or facilitate school credit through the program, and that there is no guarantee of continuous work from week to week. Read its overview of working as an AI trainer for the current rules.

Other platforms exist

Many other companies and vendors hire people for data labelling, evaluation and specialist review. The same checks below apply to any of them.

How to compare any AI training platform

  1. Eligibility: Is your country and work-authorization status accepted?
  2. Engagement type: Contractor or employee? Per hour or per task? Any minimum or guaranteed hours?
  3. Pay: Is the rate stated on the actual listing, and how and when is it paid?
  4. Screening: What interview, test and identity checks are required, and how long does it take?
  5. Agreement terms: What does it say about intellectual property, confidentiality and exclusivity? Read before you sign.
  6. Project availability: Is work continuous, or does it come in waves?
  7. Reputation: What do current contractors say, and does the company publish clear terms and support contacts?
  8. Safety: Does it ever ask you for money? If so, stop. See how to spot AI training and remote job scams.

Concerns people raise

Public coverage of this sector notes that some contractors worry about unclear agreements on rights to their work, about the lack of guaranteed hours, and about helping to train systems that could change their own professions. Weigh these honestly against the flexibility and income potential, and read every agreement carefully.

Frequently asked questions

Is Mercor legit?

Mercor is a real company that connects experts with AI companies, according to public coverage. As with any platform, read its current terms, apply only through its official site and never pay to apply or get paid.

Is micro1 legit?

micro1 publishes its own guides and a jobs site describing AI training and evaluation roles. Apply only on its official site, check the pay and terms on each listing, and never pay a fee.

What is Handshake AI?

It is Handshake's program for students, graduates and professionals who do paid, part-time, remote work training and evaluating AI models. Check its current eligibility rules for your country and status.

Which platform pays the most?

Pay depends on the project, your expertise and the platform, and it changes. Compare the stated rate on each actual listing instead of relying on a general claim.

Can I work on several platforms at once?

Often yes for independent contractors, but read each agreement for exclusivity or conflict-of-interest clauses first.

How long does it take to get approved?

It varies from days to several weeks, and approval does not guarantee projects. Plan for waiting periods.
