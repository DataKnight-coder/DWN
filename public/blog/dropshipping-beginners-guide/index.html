Dropshipping is a way of selling products you do not hold in stock. When a customer orders from you, a supplier ships the product directly to them. You set the price, attract the customer and handle customer service; the supplier stores, packs and ships. It is simple to describe and hard to do well, because margins are thin and you depend on a supplier you do not control. This guide explains how it works, how the three most common routes (your own Shopify store, eBay and Amazon) differ, and what to check in Canada and elsewhere before you start. There is no guaranteed profit.

How dropshipping works

Shopify describes the model this way: a store makes a sale, forwards the order to a supplier, and the supplier ships to the customer. You buy inventory only as needed from a wholesaler or manufacturer. You remain responsible for the customer experience, including answering questions, tracking orders and handling returns.

Advantages: low upfront inventory cost, flexible location and the ability to test products.

Trade-offs: lower margin per sale, heavy reliance on marketing, competition from others selling the same products and dependence on your supplier's stock, speed and quality.

Three routes compared

Your own store (for example, Shopify). You build and brand a store and connect to suppliers through apps or direct agreements. You control the customer relationship and the experience, but you must bring your own traffic through marketing. See the Shopify notes below.

eBay. You list products on a large marketplace with existing buyers. eBay allows dropshipping from a supplier who ships directly, but it does not allow listing an item and then buying it from another retailer, such as Amazon or Walmart, to ship to your customer. See eBay dropshipping: what is allowed.

Amazon. Amazon allows dropshipping only if you are the seller of record, and your packaging, packing slips and invoices must not name another seller. Buying from another retailer and having it shipped to your customer is prohibited. See Amazon dropshipping rules.

Shopify in practice

Shopify's own material outlines a starting path: pick a niche, research competitors, choose a supplier, create your store and market it. It suggests judging suppliers on delivery speed, inventory reliability and returns policy, and ordering samples to check quality. Shopify also offers a supplier network and apps that connect to suppliers. Details and prices change, so use Shopify's dropshipping help for the current options.

Choosing a supplier

  • Ask for a written agreement that covers stock, shipping times, returns and who pays for damaged goods.
  • Order samples before you list a product.
  • Check delivery times honestly and display them accurately to customers.
  • Understand returns. Customers return to you, so know what the supplier will accept.
  • Beware of suppliers or courses that promise easy money. No supplier can promise your sales.

Canada and elsewhere: money, tax and customs

If you operate from Canada, keep records of income and expenses from the start. The Canada Revenue Agency explains the tax obligations of people earning through digital platforms, and you may need to register for GST/HST once your taxable sales pass the threshold; see the CRA's registration guidance. Goods that come from outside the country may attract duties and taxes at the border, so check the Canada Border Services Agency before you promise delivery prices and times. If you hold a work permit, make sure running a business is allowed under your conditions. Rules in other countries differ.

A simple 30-day test

  1. Choose one product category you understand and can explain.
  2. Find two possible suppliers and order a sample from each.
  3. List a small number of products with accurate delivery times.
  4. Spend a small, fixed budget on testing, and track every cost, including your time.
  5. Review real orders, returns and customer questions before you scale.

Frequently asked questions

Is dropshipping legal?

Yes, it is a legal business model in general. You must still follow tax, consumer-protection and platform rules in the places where you sell, and any rules attached to your immigration status.

Is dropshipping profitable?

It can be, but it is not guaranteed. Margins are often thin, and marketing and returns cost money. Track every cost before you scale.

Do I need a registered business?

Requirements depend on where you live and how much you sell. Shopify lists registering your business as a step to consider over time. Ask the relevant authority or a qualified professional for your situation.

Can I dropship from Amazon to eBay?

No. Both platforms prohibit buying an item from another retailer after the sale and having it shipped to your customer. See our eBay and Amazon guides.

Which is best for a beginner: Shopify, eBay or Amazon?

It depends on your budget and skills. A marketplace gives you existing buyers but strict rules; your own store gives control but requires marketing. Start small on one route and learn it well.

How long do customers wait for dropshipped orders?

It depends on the supplier and location. Always show honest delivery times, because slow or unclear shipping is a common source of complaints.
