Do Landlords and Employers Check Your Credit in Canada?

Who checks credit, what consent means, and what to do if your history is thin.

Many newcomers are surprised when a landlord asks for permission to run a credit check, or when a job process includes one. This guide explains who usually checks credit and what the rules say about your consent. Rules vary by province, so treat this as a starting point.

Landlords

Many landlords ask for a credit check, along with references and proof of income, because they want to know you will pay the rent. The Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada explains that a prospective landlord needs your consent before sharing your information with a credit reporting agency. See its page on privacy in the landlord and tenant relationship. Provincial rules add detail, so check your province's tenancy information too.

Read any consent form before you sign it, and check your own credit report first so you know what a landlord will see. See how to build credit in Canada as a newcomer.

Employers

Some employers run credit checks for certain roles, usually those with financial responsibility or access to sensitive information, such as banking and finance. Most provinces have credit-reporting rules that require notice or consent, and some add further limits. An employer usually asks for your written consent. Ask whether a check is part of the process and why the role needs it.

Other places credit matters

  • Mobile phone plans. Many carriers check credit before they offer a plan.
  • Loans, lines of credit and mortgages. Lenders rely heavily on your credit history.
  • Credit card only services. Some car rentals, hotels and parking services ask for a credit card instead of a debit card.

What most people get wrong

  • Signing a consent form without reading it.
  • Not checking their own report before a landlord does.
  • Giving up on a rental or a job because of thin credit, instead of offering other proof.
  • Believing a credit score is the only thing that matters.

Frequently asked questions

Can a landlord run a credit check without my permission?

In Canada, a landlord generally needs your consent before running a credit check. The privacy commissioner explains this, and provincial rules add details.

Do all employers check credit?

No. It is mostly for roles with financial responsibility or sensitive information, and rules vary by province.

What if I have no Canadian credit history?

Ask what other proof the landlord or employer accepts, and start building credit.

Can I be refused because of bad credit?

Landlords and some employers can consider credit as one factor. Rules and protections vary by province, so check yours.

Educational note: This guide gives general information, not legal advice. Rules vary by province, so check your provincial tenancy and privacy sources.
