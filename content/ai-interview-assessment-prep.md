# How to Prepare for an AI Training Interview or Assessment

Many AI-training and data-evaluation platforms screen applicants with an automated interview, a short quiz or a written task before offering any project. Each platform sets its own process, and it changes, so this guide explains what the screening usually tests and how to prepare honestly. It does not guarantee an offer, and no preparation can.

## What screening can look like

Platform documentation and applicant write-ups describe a mix of the following. Not every platform uses all of them:

- **An AI-led interview.** You answer questions on video or by voice while software records and scores your answers. Mercor's own help glossary lists AI interviews among its assessment types, and micro1 describes an AI interview as its main screening step.
- **Quizzes.** Multiple-choice questions on your subject area, reasoning or language.
- **Written tasks.** You evaluate AI answers, rewrite a response or explain your reasoning in plain language.
- **Profile review.** Your resume, education and relevant experience, checked against open projects.

Because each platform can change its steps at any time, always read the instructions inside your own application and follow the platform's official guidance, not a third party's summary.

## What the screening is really checking

1. **Your subject knowledge.** Domain experts (health, law, finance, coding, languages, education) are often in the highest demand because they can judge whether an AI answer is correct.
2. **Clear written communication.** Many tasks require you to explain why an answer is good or poor.
3. **Reliability.** Following instructions exactly, meeting deadlines and keeping your word on quality.
4. **Honesty.** Platforms commonly prohibit using AI to produce your answers during an assessment. Read the rules before you start.

## How to prepare

1. **Update your resume first.** Use plain, factual wording and match your real experience. See [Canadian resume format for newcomers](/blog/canadian-resume-format-newcomers/) for a clean layout.
2. **Review your own subject area.** Refresh the core concepts you would be asked to judge.
3. **Practise explaining your reasoning in writing.** Take an answer and write three sentences on why it is right or wrong.
4. **Test your setup.** Quiet room, stable internet, working camera and microphone, and a charged device.
5. **Read the instructions twice.** Many people lose points by skipping details such as word limits or required formats.
6. **Answer in your own words.** If the platform bans outside help or AI tools during the test, follow that rule.

## During an AI interview

- Speak at a steady pace and finish each answer before the timer ends.
- Give a direct answer first, then a short example from real experience.
- If you do not know something, say so and describe how you would find out. Do not invent facts.
- Keep your face and documents visible if the platform asks for identity checks, and only upload them inside the official platform.

## Staying safe

Real platforms never charge applicants for tests, training or access. Anyone who asks you to pay, buy equipment from them, or move the conversation to a private chat app is a red flag. Read [how to spot AI training and remote job scams](/blog/ai-training-job-scams/) before sharing any documents.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do all AI training platforms use an AI interview?

No. Some use quizzes or written tasks. Check the steps in your own application.

### How long do these assessments take?

It varies by platform and role. The instructions inside your application tell you the time limit.

### Can I retake an assessment?

It depends on the platform. Read their help pages. Do not create duplicate accounts, because platforms may prohibit it.

### Will passing the assessment guarantee projects or income?

No. Task availability varies, and some applicants pass screening but wait for work. Pay and availability are set by each platform.

### Is it okay to use AI tools during the assessment?

Only if the platform's rules allow it. Many do not. Read the instructions before you begin.

### Do I need a degree?

Requirements differ by platform and project. Some projects look for specific expertise, which may or may not require a degree. Check the project description.

**Educational note:** This guide gives general information about how platforms usually screen applicants. It is not an endorsement of any platform and cannot promise acceptance or income. Platforms change their rules often, so confirm details on their official sites.
