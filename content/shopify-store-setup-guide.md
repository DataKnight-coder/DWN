# How to Set Up a Shopify Store: A Practical Launch Checklist

Setting up a store is the easy part; getting customers is the hard part. Still, a careful setup avoids the mistakes that cost new sellers money: wrong tax settings, unclear policies, surprise shipping costs and checkout problems found by your first customer. This checklist follows Shopify's own setup guidance, with extra attention to the points Canadian and international sellers often miss. It is general information, not tax or legal advice, and Shopify's help pages are the source of truth for current features and prices.

If you are still deciding on a business model, read [dropshipping for beginners](/blog/dropshipping-beginners-guide/) first.

## Before you open the admin

- **Decide what you sell and to whom.** One clear product category is easier to explain, photograph and support than many.
- **Choose how you will fulfil orders.** Your own stock, a supplier that ships for you, or print-on-demand each change your costs and timelines.
- **Check your own status and rules.** Make sure running a business is allowed under your immigration status if you hold a permit, and keep income records from day one. See the CRA's [guidance for people who earn through digital platforms](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/programs/about-canada-revenue-agency-cra/compliance/platform-economy/understanding-tax-obligations.html).

## The setup checklist

[Shopify's general checklist](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/intro-to-shopify/initial-setup/new-to-shopify-checklists/general-checklist) walks through these steps. Work through them in order:

1. **Store name and business details:** enter accurate contact and business information.
2. **Currency and units:** set the currency you will sell in and your weight units.
3. **Products:** add clear titles, honest descriptions, good photos and accurate prices.
4. **Shipping:** set rates and delivery estimates you can actually meet.
5. **Taxes:** configure the taxes that apply to you (details below).
6. **Payments:** set up a payment provider so you can get paid.
7. **Store policies:** add refund, shipping and privacy policies.
8. **Checkout and emails:** review what customers see and receive.
9. **Test and launch:** place a test order, check mobile and confirm everything works before you share the link.

## Taxes: do not rely on defaults

Shopify's Canada setup guide explains that it applies default tax settings but that you must verify the rates your business needs to charge, because Shopify cannot determine the correct rates for you. Shopify also states that it does not file or remit your taxes for you unless you use its separate automated tax filing product. In Canada, the right approach is to check the CRA's [GST/HST registration guidance](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html) and, ideally, speak to a local tax professional. See Shopify's [tax help](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/taxes) and its [Canada quick setup guide](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/intro-to-shopify/go-digital-canada/start/quick-setup-guide).

## Shipping that matches reality

Customers judge you on delivery. Shopify describes options including its own discounted carrier rates in Canada, flat or free shipping, location-based rates and connecting your own carrier accounts. Whichever you choose, state delivery times honestly. If you dropship from a supplier abroad, remember that duties and taxes may apply at the border; check the [Canada Border Services Agency](https://www.cbsa-asfc.gc.ca/) and tell customers what to expect.

## Policies you must have

Add refund, shipping and privacy policies under your store's legal settings, starting from Shopify's templates and adjusting them to match what you really do. Policies appear in your checkout footer. Clear policies reduce disputes, and they should say who pays return shipping and how long refunds take. If you collect customer data or run marketing, read your local privacy rules as well.

## Payments

A new store typically sets up a payment provider so customers can pay by card and wallets. Finish the identity and bank verification early, because payouts depend on a fully completed account. Shopify's pages describe the current providers and fees, which change, so check them for your country.

## Test before launch

- Place a test order and read every email the customer receives.
- Check the whole checkout on a phone.
- Confirm shipping options, tax lines and discount codes.
- Read your policies as a customer would.
- Ask a friend to buy something and tell you what confused them.

## After launch: traffic is the real work

A store with no visitors earns nothing. Plan how people will find you: search, social content, email, partnerships or ads. Start with small tests and track what each customer costs to acquire. Compare the model with selling on [eBay](/blog/ebay-dropshipping-guide/) or [Amazon](/blog/amazon-dropshipping-rules/), where buyers already exist but the rules are stricter, and with [affiliate marketing](/blog/affiliate-marketing-canada/), which needs no inventory.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long does it take to set up a Shopify store?

A basic store can be built in a day or two, but products, policies, tax settings and testing usually take longer. Allow time to do it carefully before you launch.

### Do I need a registered business to open a store?

Requirements depend on where you live and how much you sell. Check your local rules and speak to a qualified professional. Shopify lists registering your business as a step to consider as you grow.

### Does Shopify handle my sales tax?

Shopify applies default settings and tools, but it states that you must verify the rates and that it does not file or remit your taxes for you unless you use its separate filing product.

### Can I sell from Canada to other countries?

Often yes, but customs, duties, shipping and tax rules vary by country. Research each market you sell into, and show customers an accurate total.

### What should I put in my refund policy?

Say what can be returned, within how many days, who pays return shipping and how refunds are made. Use Shopify's template as a starting point and match it to your real process.

### Is a Shopify store enough to make money?

No. It is a tool. Income depends on your product, price, marketing and service, and nothing is guaranteed.
