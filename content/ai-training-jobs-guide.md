# AI Training Jobs: What They Are and How to Get Started

AI training jobs are paid, usually part-time and remote tasks in which people review, rank, write, correct and evaluate the output of AI systems so those systems improve. The work is open to people in many fields, not only technologists, and it is not limited to any one country or background. It is also not a guaranteed income. This guide explains what the work involves, how screening usually works, how to prepare and what to watch out for, using the platforms' own descriptions wherever possible.

## What "AI training" work actually is

Large language models learn partly from human judgment. In a common method called reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF), people compare or score model answers, those preferences are used to train a reward model, and the reward model is then used to fine-tune the AI. [A widely cited research paper explains the method](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.12773). In practice, the tasks people are paid for include:

- **Ranking and comparing responses:** choosing which of two answers is better and explaining why.
- **Rating and verifying outputs:** checking answers for accuracy, tone, safety and whether they follow instructions.
- **Writing prompts and ideal answers:** creating questions and model responses in your subject area or language.
- **Reviewing and correcting code:** fixing or explaining auto-generated scripts.
- **Specialist evaluation:** applying professional knowledge, for example in law, finance, medicine, science, teaching or translation, to judge whether an answer is correct.
- **Safety and red-team testing:** trying to find where a model fails or behaves badly, following strict guidelines.

## Who can do it

Entry-level projects often ask mainly for careful reading, attention to detail and the ability to follow precise written instructions. Expert projects ask for demonstrable experience or qualifications in a field, and some require tools or prior annotation experience. Most work is done on your own computer with a reliable internet connection, in English or sometimes other languages.

Eligibility differs by platform, country and project. Some programmes are open only to students, graduates or professionals in certain locations, with work-authorization conditions, so always read the current terms. ## How you are usually engaged

Most roles are independent contractor work, paid per hour or per task, often with no guaranteed hours. A platform's own listing may say there are no fixed working hours and no guaranteed volume of assignments, and that contractors are responsible for their own taxes and expenses. That is useful flexibility for some people and uncertainty for others. Think of it as a way to add income around your life, not as a replacement for a stable job until you have real experience of the volume available to you.

## What does it pay?

Pay is set per project and varies enormously with the subject, the level of expertise required, the platform and sometimes your location. Examples in public listings range from low hourly rates for simple labelling to much higher rates for specialist experts. Treat any headline number, including numbers in videos and social posts, as an example and not a promise. Check the specific listing and the payment terms before you start, and never assume a rate will be available all year.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is AI training work?

It is paid work evaluating and improving AI outputs: ranking answers, checking facts, writing examples, correcting code and testing for safety. The goal is to make AI systems more accurate and useful.

### Do I need a degree or technical background?

Not always. Some entry-level projects emphasise careful reading and following instructions. Expert projects ask for qualifications or experience in the subject, which can be professional experience as well as a degree.

### Can I do AI training work from Canada or outside the United States?

Often yes, but it depends on the platform and project. Some programs restrict eligibility by country or work authorization. Read each platform's current eligibility page before applying.

### Is it a full-time job?

Usually not. It is typically contract work with no guaranteed weekly hours, so availability can change from week to week.

### How much does it pay?

It varies widely by project and expertise level. No amount is guaranteed. Check each listing and treat quoted rates in videos or posts as examples only.

### Do I have to pay to join or get access to tasks?

No. A legitimate platform does not charge fees to apply, test, train or receive tasks or payment. A request for money is a major warning sign.

### Which platform is best?

There is no single best option. It depends on your expertise, location, availability and the terms you are comfortable with. See our [platform comparison](/blog/ai-training-platforms-compared/) for what to check.
