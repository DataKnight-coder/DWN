# How to Find Legitimate Remote Jobs (and Avoid the Fakes)

Remote work can open doors for people in any city or country, but the same appeal makes it a favourite disguise for scams. Real remote roles exist, though they are a small share of all job postings, and they are often competitive. This guide explains where to look, how to filter honestly, how to judge an offer, and how to handle the money, tax and legal questions that come with working for someone remotely. It applies wherever you live, with examples from Canada.

## What counts as a remote job

- **Employee roles** with a company that lets you work from home, sometimes in a set province or time zone.
- **Contractor or freelance work,** where you invoice clients and manage your own taxes.
- **Project-based work,** such as short assignments or [AI training tasks](/blog/ai-training-jobs-guide/).
- **Hybrid roles,** which are partly remote. Read carefully, because they are not fully remote.

Be clear about which you want, because contractor work usually means no benefits, no guaranteed hours and your own tax responsibilities.

## Where to look

- **Government job boards.** In Canada, [Job Bank](https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/jobsearch/) lets you set the work location to Remote. A plain "work from home" keyword search mostly returns on-site jobs, so use the filter instead of relying on the phrase. Then sort by date to see new postings first.
- **Company career pages.** Go straight to employers you admire and check their "careers" section. This is the safest source because you know the employer is real.
- **Professional networks and specialist boards** for your field, such as software, design, accounting, customer support, writing or teaching.
- **Recruitment agencies.** Some remote roles are posted by agencies on behalf of employers. Find out who the actual employer is before you apply.
- **Your own contacts.** Many remote jobs are filled through people who already know your work.

## Match the role to your skills

Remote roles are more often professional or technical than entry-level, because employers cannot supervise you in person. Common fields include software and IT, customer support, content and communications, accounting and finance, recruitment, training and instructional design. If you are a career changer, build proof of skill: a small portfolio, a project, a certificate in a real field, or a clear record of results.

## Prepare a remote-ready application

1. **A resume with specific results,** not just duties.
2. **A short, tailored message** that shows you read the posting.
3. **Evidence of remote readiness:** a quiet workspace, reliable internet, a decent computer and a plan for time zones.
4. **Clear communication.** Remote employers hire people who write and explain well.
5. **References and work samples** you can share when asked.

## How to judge whether an offer is real

Treat any remote offer as unproven until you verify it. Warning signs include:

- **You are asked to pay** for training, equipment, a background check or access to tasks. A real employer does not charge you to work.
- **You are asked to move money** through your own account or to accept a cheque and send some back.
- **The job was never applied for,** and a recruiter contacts you first on a messaging app promising immediate hiring.
- **A free email address** is used instead of a company domain, or the company has no verifiable address or website.
- **Pay is far above normal** for very little work, or the posting is vague about duties.
- **Pressure and urgency,** such as "reply within an hour".

Read our full guide on [how to spot AI training and remote job scams](/blog/ai-training-job-scams/), and check the Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre's [job scam page](https://antifraudcentre-centreantifraude.ca/scams-fraudes/job-emploi-eng.htm). Verify the employer on its official website and phone number, never through contact details in the message.

## Money, taxes and work authorization

- **Employee or contractor?** Employees usually have tax deducted at source. Contractors invoice and report their own income. Keep records either way.
- **Where you live matters.** Working remotely for a foreign company can raise tax and legal questions in both places. Ask a qualified adviser before you sign.
- **Work permits.** If you hold a temporary status in Canada, remote work for any employer may still be limited by your permit. [IRCC explains that permits may restrict the type of work or the employer](https://www.ircc.canada.ca/english/helpcentre/answer.asp?qnum=169&top=17). Check your own documents.
- **Reporting income.** The CRA explains the [tax obligations of people who earn through digital platforms](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/programs/about-canada-revenue-agency-cra/compliance/platform-economy/understanding-tax-obligations.html).

## Frequently asked questions

### Where can I find legitimate remote jobs?

Start with company career pages, professional networks and reputable job boards, and use filters for remote work. In Canada, Job Bank lets you filter by remote work location.

### Are "work from home" jobs real?

Some are, but the phrase also attracts scams. Verify the employer, never pay to apply or work, and be cautious about unsolicited offers on messaging apps.

### Do I need experience to get a remote job?

Many remote roles ask for proven skills, because employers cannot supervise you in person. Build a portfolio, samples or certifications that show what you can do.

### Can I work remotely for a company in another country?

Often yes, but tax, legal and immigration rules may apply in both places. Get advice before you sign an agreement.

### Is contract work the same as a job?

No. Contractors usually have no benefits or guaranteed hours and handle their own taxes. Make sure you understand which you are being offered.

### What if a remote job asks me to pay for equipment?

Treat it as a major red flag. Legitimate employers provide equipment or reimburse you through normal processes. They do not ask you to send money first.
