# Do Landlords and Employers Check Your Credit in Canada?

Many newcomers are surprised when a landlord asks for permission to run a credit check, or when a job process includes one. This guide explains who usually checks credit, what the rules say about your consent, and what to do if your Canadian credit history is thin. Rules vary by province, so treat this as a starting point.

## Landlords

Many landlords ask for a credit check, along with references and proof of income, because they want to know you will pay the rent. The Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada explains that a prospective landlord needs your consent before sharing your information with a credit reporting agency. See its page on [privacy in the landlord and tenant relationship](https://priv.gc.ca/en/privacy-topics/landlords-and-tenants/privacy-in-the-landlord-and-tenant-relationship). Provincial rules add detail, so check your province's tenancy information too.

**What to do**
- Ask what the landlord checks and why, and read any consent form before you sign it.
- Check your own credit report first, so you know what they will see. See [how to build credit in Canada as a newcomer](/blog/build-credit-canada-newcomers/).
- If you have little Canadian credit history, offer other proof: reference letters, proof of income, a letter from your employer, or a guarantor, if the landlord accepts one.

## Employers

Some employers run credit checks for certain roles, usually those with financial responsibility or access to sensitive information, such as banking and finance. Practice and law vary: most provinces have credit-reporting rules that require notice or consent, and some provinces have additional limits. An employer usually asks for your written consent, and often only after a conditional offer.

**What to do**
- Ask whether a credit check is part of the process and why it is needed for the role.
- Read the consent form, and ask what the check will cover.
- If your history is thin, be ready to explain that you are new to Canada.

## Other places credit matters

- **Mobile phone plans.** Many carriers check credit before they offer a plan.
- **Loans, lines of credit and mortgages.** Lenders rely heavily on your credit history.
- **Credit card only services.** Some car rentals, hotels and parking services ask for a credit card instead of a debit card.

## If your credit is new or poor

- Open a beginner-friendly card, such as a secured card, and pay on time.
- Keep balances low and pay in full where you can.
- Avoid credit repair companies that charge you to fix your score.

Read the Government of Canada's guidance on [improving your credit score](https://www.canada.ca/en/financial-consumer-agency/services/credit-reports-score/improve-credit-score.html).

## What most people get wrong

- Signing a consent form without reading it.
- Not checking their own report before a landlord does.
- Giving up on a rental or a job because of thin credit, instead of offering other proof.
- Believing a credit score is the only thing that matters.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can a landlord run a credit check without my permission?

In Canada, a landlord generally needs your consent before running a credit check. The privacy commissioner explains this, and provincial rules add details.

### Do all employers check credit?

No. It is mostly for roles with financial responsibility or sensitive information, and rules vary by province.

### Will a credit check hurt my score?

A check that you or a landlord run for your own application can show on your report, so do not authorise many checks without a reason. Ask the bureau how it records inquiries.

### What if I have no Canadian credit history?

Offer alternative proof, such as references, proof of income and a guarantor if accepted, and start building credit.

### Can I be refused because of bad credit?

Landlords and some employers can consider credit as one factor. Rules and protections vary by province, so check yours.

**Educational note:** This guide gives general information, not legal advice. Rules vary by province, so check your provincial tenancy and privacy sources.
