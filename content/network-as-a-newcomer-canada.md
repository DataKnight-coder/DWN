# How to Build a Professional Network as a Newcomer in Canada

In Canada, many jobs are filled through people, not job boards. If you arrived without a network, that can feel like a wall. It is not. A network is built one conversation at a time, and you can start with none. This guide gives a simple starting plan that works for professionals from any country.

## Why networking matters here

Employers often prefer to hire someone a colleague recommends, and many roles are never widely advertised. Networking is not about asking strangers for jobs. It is about learning how your field works in Canada, getting advice, and letting people see what you can do.

## Start with the people you already know

List everyone you know in Canada: neighbours, former classmates, people from your community, colleagues from past work, people from your place of worship or clubs. Tell them clearly what kind of work you are looking for. A warm contact with a clear request beats a cold message every time.

## Use free and low-cost places to meet people

- **Settlement and employment agencies.** Many offer free mentoring and networking events for newcomers.
- **Professional associations.** Many run local chapters, events and student or newcomer memberships.
- **Volunteering.** It builds Canadian experience, references and relationships at the same time.
- **Online.** A clear LinkedIn profile makes it easier for people to find you. See [LinkedIn profile optimization for the Canadian job market](/blog/linkedin-profile-canada-job-market/).

## Have short, useful conversations

A good request is specific and small: "I am an accountant who moved here recently. Could I ask you three questions about how people enter your field in Canada?" People usually say yes to a short, respectful request. Thank them afterwards, and tell them what you learned.

## Give before you ask

Share a useful article, make an introduction, or offer a skill. Networking is a relationship, not a transaction, and people remember who was helpful.

## Protect yourself while networking

Be careful with strangers who offer jobs but ask for money, documents, or a move to a private chat app. Read [how to spot job scams](/blog/ai-training-job-scams/).

## What most newcomers get wrong

- Waiting until they are desperate for a job to start meeting people.
- Asking for a job in the first message.
- Going to events but never following up.
- Doing it alone, with no one to practise with.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I network if I know nobody in Canada?

Start with settlement agencies, professional associations, volunteering and online communities, and ask the few people you do know to introduce you to others.

### Is it rude to ask for an informational conversation?

No. Most people are happy to give twenty minutes to someone who is respectful, specific and grateful.

### How many people should I contact?

Quality matters more than numbers. A few well-prepared conversations a week, with follow-up, beat dozens of unanswered messages.

### Can networking replace applying for jobs?

No. Do both. Networking helps you learn, get advice and sometimes hear about roles before they are posted.

### Is LinkedIn enough?

It helps, but real conversations matter more. Use LinkedIn to make yourself easy to find and to follow up.
