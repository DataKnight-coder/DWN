# Amazon Dropshipping Rules: Seller of Record and What You Must Do

Amazon allows dropshipping, but only on strict terms. The central idea is that you must be the "seller of record": the customer must see you, and only you, as the seller on every document in the box. This guide explains that rule, what is and is not allowed, how it compares with eBay, and what to do before you try it. It is general information based on Amazon's published guidance for sellers; check Amazon's current policy pages before you act.

## The seller-of-record rule

[Amazon's guidance on dropshipping](https://sell.amazon.com/blog/dropshipping-on-amazon) says that if you use dropshipping you need an agreement with your supplier that makes you the seller of record, meaning you own the products before customers buy them. As the seller of record you are responsible for:

- **Setting the price** of the product.
- **Recording the sale as revenue** in your business.
- **Handling sales tax** where it applies.
- **Customer service and returns,** which you must accept and process under Amazon's seller agreement and policies.

## What your packaging must look like

Amazon expects that packaging, packing slips and invoices do not reference any third party. That means:

- **Your name is the seller's name** on every packing slip and invoice.
- **No supplier name, logo or contact details** on the box, packing slip, invoice or inserts.
- **Remove any documents** that identify a different seller or supplier before the order is shipped, or arrange with your supplier to ship without them.

If a customer sees another seller's name or receipt, it creates confusion, which is why Amazon enforces the rule.

## What is not allowed

Buying a product from another retailer or marketplace and having it shipped directly to your Amazon customer is prohibited when the shipment does not name you as the seller of record or when another party appears on the paperwork. That includes the common but risky idea of reselling products from other retail websites by sending them directly to your buyer.

Amazon says violations can restrict your ability to sell through its seller-fulfilled network, and repeated violations can put your account at risk. Policies can change, so confirm the current wording on Amazon's seller pages.

## A compliant setup, step by step

1. **Choose a real supplier** such as a manufacturer, wholesaler or distributor, not a retail store.
2. **Sign a supplier agreement** that confirms you own the goods before the sale, that shipments carry your name only, and that sets shipping times and returns.
3. **Test the paperwork** by ordering a sample to confirm there are no third-party names or prices on the package.
4. **Set your price and handling time** using the true delivery time from your supplier.
5. **Plan returns,** because you remain responsible for accepting them.
6. **Keep records** of purchases, sales and tax from the first order. In Canada, see the CRA's [platform-economy guidance](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/programs/about-canada-revenue-agency-cra/compliance/platform-economy/understanding-tax-obligations.html).

## Amazon compared with eBay and your own store

Amazon is the strictest of the three on packaging and the seller-of-record rule. eBay allows supplier-shipped orders but prohibits buying from another retailer after a sale; see [eBay dropshipping](/blog/ebay-dropshipping-guide/). A store you run yourself gives you the most control but requires your own marketing; see our [beginner comparison](/blog/dropshipping-beginners-guide/).

## Do not confuse this with Amazon's affiliate program

Selling products on Amazon as a seller is different from earning commission as an Amazon Associate by recommending products. The affiliate route has its own rules; see [affiliate programs in Canada](/blog/best-affiliate-programs-canada/).

## Frequently asked questions

### Does Amazon allow dropshipping?

Yes, but only if you are the seller of record, you own the goods before the sale, and your packaging and paperwork do not identify any other seller or supplier.

### Can I buy from another retailer and have it shipped to my Amazon customer?

No. Buying from another retailer and having it shipped directly to your customer is prohibited when you are not the seller of record or when another party appears on the paperwork.

### What does seller of record mean?

It means you own the product before it is sold, you set the price, you record the revenue and you handle sales tax and returns, and customers see you as the seller.

### What happens if I break the rule?

Your ability to sell through the seller-fulfilled program can be restricted, and your account can be put at risk. Read the current policy before you start.

### Do I still handle returns?

Yes. As the seller of record you must accept and process customer returns under Amazon's policies.

### Can I dropship Amazon products onto eBay?

No. Both platforms prohibit that practice. See our [eBay guide](/blog/ebay-dropshipping-guide/).
