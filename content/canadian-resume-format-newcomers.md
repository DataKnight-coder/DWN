# Canadian Resume Format for Newcomers: What to Include and Leave Out

A Canadian resume is short, plain and focused on what you can do for the employer. If you have worked abroad, the biggest changes are what to remove (photo, age, marital status), how to order the page, and how to present overseas experience so a hiring manager can read it quickly. This guide follows the advice published by the Government of Canada's Job Bank and applies to newcomers from any country.

## What a Canadian resume looks like

Job Bank's resume guidance, together with common newcomer-employment advice, points to the same basic shape:

1. **Contact details.** Your name, phone number, email address and city and province. A LinkedIn link is fine.
2. **A short summary.** Two or three lines naming the role you want and your strongest relevant skills.
3. **Work experience.** Most recent job first (reverse chronological order). For each job: title, employer, city, dates and three to five short bullet points.
4. **Education and training.** Degrees, diplomas, certificates and licences, with the country where you earned them.
5. **Skills.** Software, languages and tools that match the job posting.

Job Bank recommends putting the emphasis on your most recent experience, and trimming or cutting roles older than about 15 years.

## What to leave out

- **A photo.** Job Bank states that including a photo is not the norm in Canada.
- **Age, marital status, nationality or religion.** These details can expose you to unfair bias, and employers do not need them.
- **Your Social Insurance Number.** Job Bank advises never to put it on a resume. Share it only with an employer after you accept a job offer, for payroll.
- **References on the page.** Keep references on a separate sheet and send them only when an employer asks.

## How long should it be?

Job Bank suggests keeping a resume to two pages, because an employer may spend only about 30 seconds skimming it. One page is usually enough for early-career applicants.

## Presenting overseas experience

- **Describe the work, not just the title.** Titles do not always translate. "Senior Officer" means little; "Managed a 12-person billing team and reduced payment errors" is clear.
- **Use numbers where you honestly can.** Job Bank recommends firm figures such as team size, sales growth or time saved. Never invent them.
- **Name the country and explain unfamiliar employers.** One short phrase such as "(national bank, 4,000 employees)" helps.
- **List credentials accurately.** If you have had your education assessed, say so. See [how to get foreign credentials recognized in Alberta](/blog/foreign-credential-recognition-alberta/).

## Tailor every application

Read the posting and match its wording where it is true for you. Many employers use applicant tracking systems (ATS), software that scans resumes for keywords before a person reads them. Use standard section headings and a simple layout, and avoid tables, text boxes and graphics that software can misread.

## Free tools

Job Bank's [Resume Builder](https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/findajob/resume-builder) is free with an account. It offers a traditional template and a template for recent graduates and first-time job seekers, lets you search for pre-written job duties by title, and exports to PDF or RTF. Their guide [How to write a good resume](https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/findajob/resources/write-good-resume) covers the same rules in more detail.

## A simple checklist before you apply

- Does the first third of the page show why you fit this job?
- Are dates, titles and spelling consistent?
- Is it two pages or fewer, saved as a PDF (unless the employer asks for another format)?
- Is the file name clear, such as `Firstname-Lastname-Resume.pdf`?
- Have you removed the photo and personal details listed above?

## Frequently asked questions

### Should I put a photo on my resume in Canada?

No. Job Bank says it is not the norm in Canada to include a photo.

### How long should a Canadian resume be?

Job Bank suggests two pages at most. One page is common for people early in their career.

### Do I need a cover letter?

Read the posting. If it asks for one, send it, and keep it short and specific to the role. If it does not, a strong resume is usually enough.

### Should I include references?

Not on the resume. Prepare a separate list and provide it when an employer asks.

### Can I use my overseas job titles?

Yes, but add a short plain-language description of what you did. Canadian employers may not know the title or the company.

### Is a free resume template safe to use?

Use a simple template with standard headings. Job Bank's own Resume Builder is a free government option. Avoid any service that asks you to pay before showing you anything.

**Educational note:** This guide gives general information, not career, legal or immigration advice. Employer expectations vary by industry and role.
