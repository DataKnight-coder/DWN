# Affiliate Marketing in Canada: A Beginner's Guide

Affiliate marketing means recommending a product or service and receiving a commission when a qualifying action happens through your referral link. It can be a useful income pathway for a person who understands an audience and can help that audience make a considered decision. It is not guaranteed income, and a link alone is rarely useful content.

If you are choosing among several possible [online side hustles in Canada](/blog/online-side-hustles-canada/), affiliate marketing works best when you are willing to research products, explain trade-offs and maintain trust over time.

## Start with an audience, not a commission rate

Choose a specific reader and a problem you can discuss from knowledge or careful research. For example, a career educator might compare learning tools for people changing fields. A business educator might explain software that helps a small team organize its work. You should be able to answer why a recommendation fits a reader, when it does not, and what it costs.

A useful piece of content can be a comparison, tutorial or practical checklist. Explain selection criteria first, then show how each option meets them. Revisit content when pricing, features or terms change. Avoid presenting a product you have not assessed as if you personally tested it.

## How to evaluate affiliate programs for a Canadian audience

“Best affiliate program” depends on the audience and product, not a universal ranking. Check:

- **Audience fit:** Is the product available to people in Canada, and does it solve their actual problem?
- **Eligibility:** Does the program accept your type of website or channel? Some programs review content quality or audience size.
- **Commission terms:** What counts as a qualified referral, when is it paid, and can a sale be reversed?
- **Customer experience:** Can readers understand the offer, currency, support and cancellation terms before buying?
- **Disclosure requirements:** What wording does the program require in addition to Canadian advertising rules?

Two programs you can examine directly are [Amazon.ca Associates](https://associates.amazon.ca/) for relevant products and [Shopify's affiliate program](https://www.shopify.com/affiliates) for an audience interested in commerce. They serve different audiences and have their own eligibility and payment terms. Their inclusion is an example for research, not a recommendation to join either one. Check each provider's current terms before applying.

## Disclose the relationship clearly

The [Competition Bureau says material connections, including commissions, should be disclosed clearly and prominently](https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/en/deceptive-marketing-practices/types-deceptive-marketing-practices/influencer-marketing-and-competition-act). A discount code or affiliate link by itself may not make the connection clear. Put a plain-language disclosure close to a recommendation where readers will see it, including on mobile.

You can write, for example, “I may earn a commission if you buy through this link, at no extra cost to you,” only if that statement accurately describes your arrangement. Be honest about what you used and what you did not test. Do not make earnings or product-performance claims you cannot support.

## Measure whether your content helps

Track the questions readers ask, whether they reach the linked provider, and which explanations need improvement. The affiliate platform reports qualifying actions and commissions; your website click count alone is not a sale. Also track your time and any production costs so you can judge whether the work is worthwhile.

Keep records of commission income and related expenses. The [Canada Revenue Agency's platform-economy guidance](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/programs/about-canada-revenue-agency-cra/compliance/platform-economy/understanding-tax-obligations.html) explains that digital-platform income may create tax obligations. If you hold a work permit, check your own conditions before beginning paid activity.

## A first-month learning plan

Choose one audience and one problem. Compare two relevant products using a written checklist. Publish one useful explanation with a visible disclosure. Ask readers which questions remain unanswered. Review provider terms and actual results before deciding whether to create more content. This is a learning plan, not a promise of commissions.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is affiliate marketing legal in Canada?

Affiliate marketing can be conducted in Canada, but advertising, tax, contract and immigration rules may apply. Disclose material relationships and check your personal circumstances.

### Which affiliate program should I join first?

Choose a program whose product is genuinely relevant to your audience and whose eligibility and terms you understand. Compare official program pages rather than relying on a list of headline commission rates. Our [comparison of affiliate programs in Canada](/blog/best-affiliate-programs-canada/) shows what to check on each one.

### Does clicking an affiliate link mean I earned a commission?

No. A program defines which actions qualify and reports them in its own account. A website click is only an expression of interest.
