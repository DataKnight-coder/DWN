# How to Change Careers After Moving to Canada

Many newcomers want to move into a different field after they arrive. Some want to leave a survival job, some cannot use their old career in Canada, and some simply want a fresh start. A career change takes planning, but you do not have to guess. This guide gives the starting steps, using free Government of Canada tools.

## Start with what you already have

Before looking at new fields, write down your skills, your experience and the work you enjoyed most. Many skills carry across industries: managing people, solving problems, handling money, teaching, selling, working with technology. A career change is usually a move from one set of tasks to another, not a start from zero.

## Use the free Job Bank tools

Job Bank, run by the Government of Canada, has a career planning section that suits people who want a change:

- The [Job Transition Tool](https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/career-planning/job-transition) finds occupations that fit your current skills, and shows how long a move might take, with results you can sort by wage and job prospects.
- The [career quizzes](https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/career-planning/quizzes) help you spot paths based on your interests and abilities.
- The [job profiles](https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/career-planning/search-job-profile) explain what a job involves, what it can pay, what education it needs and what the outlook is.

## Check whether the field is regulated

Some occupations, such as health care, engineering, teaching and many trades, need approval from a regulatory body before you can work. If your target job is one of them, contact that body first. If your education was earned abroad, read [how foreign credential recognition works in Alberta](/blog/foreign-credential-recognition-alberta/).

## Update how employers see you

A career change is easier when your documents tell a clear story. Fix your resume first with [the Canadian resume format for newcomers](/blog/canadian-resume-format-newcomers/), and make your online profile match it. See [LinkedIn profile optimization for the Canadian job market](/blog/linkedin-profile-canada-job-market/).

## Build a bridge, not a leap

Most successful career changes are gradual. Common bridge steps are a short course, a certificate, volunteering, a contract or part-time role in the new field, or an informational conversation with someone already doing the job. If you want to explore a tech route, read [how to get into cybersecurity in Canada](/blog/cybersecurity-career-canada/).

## What most people get wrong

I see the same mistakes again and again from people changing careers:

- Applying everywhere instead of building a clear story for one target role.
- Taking a long course before checking that employers hire for it.
- Hiding past experience instead of showing how it transfers.
- Doing it alone, with no one to practise interviews or review applications.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I change careers without Canadian experience?

Yes, but you will need to show how your skills transfer, and you may need a bridge step such as a course, a certificate or volunteer work in the new field.

### How long does a career change take?

It depends on the field, your starting point and whether training or licensing is needed. The Job Bank Job Transition Tool shows estimated transition times for different occupations.

### Do I need to go back to school?

Not always. Some fields value experience and short certificates. Regulated professions may need specific education or exams, so check the regulatory body first.

### Is a career coach worth it?

It depends on your situation. Free resources such as Job Bank and local settlement agencies help many people. A coach or a community can add accountability. Check any coach's references and never pay for a guaranteed job.

### Where can I get free help in Canada?

Job Bank, local settlement agencies and employment centres offer free services for newcomers. Ask your city or province for the nearest one.
