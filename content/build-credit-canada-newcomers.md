# How to Build Credit in Canada as a Newcomer (and Why It Matters)

Your credit history works a bit like a report card. Lenders, landlords and other businesses use it to decide whether to trust you. If you arrive in Canada with no credit history here, you start with a blank page, and a blank page can close doors you would never expect. This guide explains why credit matters and where to start, using Government of Canada sources.

## Why credit matters, in plain words

I teach this because so many people learn about credit only after it costs them an opportunity. Here is where it commonly shows up:

- **Renting a home.** Many landlords ask for a credit check before they rent to you. Thin or poor credit can cost you the place you wanted.
- **Some jobs.** Certain roles, such as positions of financial responsibility or work in banking, may involve a credit check as part of hiring.
- **Loans.** Personal loans, business loans, car loans and student loans all depend on how lenders see your credit.
- **A mortgage.** Lenders look closely at your credit history before they approve one.
- **A line of credit.** You generally need to have built credit first.
- **A phone plan.** Many carriers check your credit before they sign you to a plan.
- **Everyday services.** Some car rentals, hotels and parking services want a credit card, not a debit card.

Every country has its own system, and the rules differ in the United States, the United Kingdom and Australia. This guide is about Canada.

## What it is, and where to start

Your **credit report** records your credit accounts and how you pay them. Your **credit score** is a number based on that record. Two main bureaus in Canada keep these records: Equifax and TransUnion. They record your activity in Canada, so a good history from another country usually does not appear, and you build a new one here. The Financial Consumer Agency of Canada (FCAC) explains the basics in [Credit report and score basics](https://www.canada.ca/en/financial-consumer-agency/services/credit-reports-score/credit-report-score-basics.html).

Three places to start:

- **Look at your own report.** FCAC explains how to [get your credit report and score](https://www.canada.ca/en/financial-consumer-agency/services/credit-reports-score/order-credit-report.html).
- **Learn about cards for beginners.** FCAC says a secured card can be an option if you have no credit history, and it names newcomers as an example. Read [choosing a credit card](https://www.canada.ca/en/financial-consumer-agency/services/credit-cards/choose-credit-card.html) before you apply.
- **Read how scores improve.** See FCAC's page on [improving your credit score](https://www.canada.ca/en/financial-consumer-agency/services/credit-reports-score/improve-credit-score.html).

## Watch out for credit repair scams

Be careful with anyone who promises to "fix" your credit for a fee, guarantees a score, or asks for your banking passwords. Legitimate help is free from the bureaus and from approved credit counselling agencies.

## What most newcomers get wrong

I see the same mistakes again and again:

- Waiting until they need a loan or a rental to start.
- Using a debit card for everything, which does not build credit.
- Missing one payment and not realising how much it costs.
- Applying for many cards at once.
- Believing credit works the same as in their home country.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long does it take to build credit in Canada?

It varies, and nobody can promise a timeline or a score. A history builds over time with on-time payments.

### Do I have a credit score when I arrive?

Usually not a Canadian one. Bureaus record Canadian activity, so you start from a blank page.

### Does a debit card build credit?

Generally no. Credit is built by borrowing and repaying, not by spending your own money.

### Is a secured credit card worth it?

FCAC says it can be an option for people with no credit history, including newcomers. Compare fees and choose a recognized issuer.

### Can I check my own credit without hurting it?

Checking your own report is different from a lender checking it when you apply. FCAC explains how to get your report.
