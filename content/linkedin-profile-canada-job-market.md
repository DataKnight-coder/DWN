# LinkedIn Profile Optimization for the Canadian Job Market

In Canada, many recruiters and hiring managers look at your LinkedIn profile before they call you. A clear profile helps them understand your experience quickly. This guide covers the parts that matter most and how newcomers can set them up. It uses LinkedIn's own help pages for the settings, and general good practice for the rest.

## Know the difference between your resume and LinkedIn

A Canadian resume leaves out a photo and personal details. LinkedIn is different: a professional photo is normal there, and your profile can be longer and more personal. The two should still tell the same story, with the same job titles, dates and skills. If you have not updated your resume yet, start with [the Canadian resume format for newcomers](/blog/canadian-resume-format-newcomers/).

## The top section: headline, photo and location

LinkedIn explains how to edit this part in [Edit the Introduction section on your profile](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a547248). It is the first thing visitors see.

- **Headline.** Say what you do and what you offer, in the words employers use for the job. A headline that names your role and key skills is clearer than one that only says you are looking for work.
- **Photo.** A clear, friendly, professional photo helps people trust your profile.
- **Location.** Use your Canadian city if you live here, so you appear in local searches.

## About, experience and skills

- **About.** Three to five short sentences: who you are, what you do best and what kind of role you want next.
- **Experience.** Use the same titles and dates as your resume. Describe results where you honestly can. Explain overseas employers in a few words, because recruiters may not know them.
- **Skills.** List the skills that appear in the job postings you want. Recruiters search by skill.

## Open to Work: choose the visibility carefully

LinkedIn's help page [Let recruiters know you're Open to Work](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a507508/let-recruiters-know-you-re-open-to-work?lang=en) explains your choices. You can share it with recruiters only, or with all members, which adds a #OpenToWork frame to your photo. If you currently have an employer, think about who might see it before you choose.

## Build your network on purpose

Connect with people in your target field, follow companies you want to work for, and send short, polite notes that explain why you are connecting. A profile that no one visits does not help. Be wary of messages from strangers who ask for money, personal documents or a move to another chat app. Read [how to spot job scams](/blog/ai-training-job-scams/).

## What most people get wrong

- A generic headline that does not match any job posting.
- A profile that contradicts the resume.
- Connecting with no message, then never speaking to anyone.
- Asking for a job in the first message to a stranger.

## Frequently asked questions

### Should I put a photo on LinkedIn if I leave it off my resume?

Yes. Canadian resumes leave the photo out, but a professional photo is normal on LinkedIn.

### What should my LinkedIn headline say?

Your target role and your main skills, in the words employers use. Avoid a headline that only says you are looking for work.

### Should I turn on Open to Work?

It depends. Sharing with recruiters only keeps it less visible. Sharing with all members adds a photo frame that anyone can see. Read LinkedIn's help page and choose based on who you do not want to see it.

### Do I need LinkedIn Premium?

Not to start. A complete profile and a thoughtful network matter more than a paid plan.

### How often should I update my profile?

Update it whenever your role, skills or goals change, and review it before you start a job search.
