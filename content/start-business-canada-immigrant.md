# How Immigrants Can Start a Business in Canada: The Basic Steps

Starting a business in Canada can be a way to use your skills, build income and create work for yourself. The basic steps are the same for citizens and newcomers, but newcomers often need to check a few extra things. This guide covers the main steps, using Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) sources, and points you to the right official sites. It is general information, not legal, tax or immigration advice.

## Check that your immigration status allows it

Some permits limit whether you can be self-employed or run a business. Before you spend money, confirm what your status allows with Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC) or a regulated immigration professional. Do not rely on a social media post.

## Test the idea before you spend

Check that real customers want what you plan to offer and will pay for it. Talk to ten potential customers, look at who else offers the same thing, and start small. Many businesses begin part-time while the owner keeps another source of income.

## Choose a structure

The main options are a sole proprietorship, a partnership and a corporation. They differ in cost, paperwork, taxes and personal responsibility for debts. A sole proprietorship is the simplest. A corporation separates the business from you but costs more to set up and run. An accountant or a free small-business advisor can help you choose.

## Register your business

Registration rules depend on your province, so check your provincial or municipal registry for naming and licence rules. For the federal tax side, the CRA explains how to [register for a Business Number](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/business-registration/business-number-program-account/how-register/resident.html). The CRA says new Business Number registrations must now be done online, through your CRA account, and that people who cannot register online, including some newcomers, may use a paper form. Read the page for the current steps. Keep your Business Number safe once you get it.

## GST/HST and income tax basics

- **Income tax.** Self-employment income must be reported on your tax return. The CRA's page on the [platform and gig economy](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/programs/about-canada-revenue-agency-cra/compliance/platform-economy/gig-economy.html) explains the reporting rules for online and self-employment income.
- **GST/HST.** The CRA explains in its [GST/HST information for registrants](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/rc4022/general-information-gst-hst-registrants.html) that you generally must register once your taxable revenues pass $30,000 over four consecutive calendar quarters. Below that you may register voluntarily. Rules have exceptions, so check the CRA page for your situation.
- **Records.** Keep invoices, receipts and bank records from day one.

## Check licences and rules for your trade

Some businesses need a licence, permit or professional registration. Ask your city and province. Food, child care, construction, health and finance are common examples.

## What most new business owners get wrong

- Spending on a website and branding before checking demand.
- Mixing personal and business money.
- Forgetting about tax until the end of the year.
- Starting alone, with nobody to ask.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can a newcomer start a business in Canada?

Often yes, but it depends on your immigration status and permit. Confirm with IRCC or a regulated immigration professional before you start.

### Do I need to incorporate?

No. Many people start as a sole proprietor. Incorporation has costs and benefits, so ask an accountant.

### When do I have to register for GST/HST?

Generally once your taxable revenues pass $30,000 over four consecutive calendar quarters. You can register voluntarily earlier. Check the CRA page for exceptions.

### Do I pay tax on small online income?

Self-employment income, including online and gig income, must be reported on your tax return, according to the CRA.

### Where can I get free help?

Ask your province or city for small-business advisory services, and local settlement agencies for newcomer business programs.
