# Side Hustles in Canada: Online and Offline Options Compared

A side hustle is any additional work you do outside your main job to earn extra income. In Canada, the right choice depends less on a trending idea and more on your time, skills, location, immigration status and how much risk you can accept. This guide compares online and offline options so you can narrow the field before you invest money or energy. It does not promise any level of income, and results vary by person.

If you already know you want to work online, our narrower guide to [online side hustles in Canada](/blog/online-side-hustles-canada/) goes deeper on digital products, freelancing and content. This page is the starting point for the wider decision.

## First, check whether you are allowed to do it

Before you compare ideas, confirm that the work is permitted for you. [IRCC explains that a work permit may limit the type of work, the employer, the location or the length of time](https://www.ircc.canada.ca/english/helpcentre/answer.asp?qnum=169&top=17). Self-employment and second jobs are treated differently depending on your status, so check your own documents and get qualified advice if the conditions are unclear. Students, temporary residents and newcomers can have very different rules.

Then check whether the activity itself is regulated. Some work, such as driving passengers, preparing food for sale, caring for children, or trades like electrical and gas work, can require licences, certifications, insurance or municipal approval. These rules vary by province and city, so look up your own municipality and provincial regulator.

## Offline and local options

Local work usually trades your time for income, which makes it easier to start and harder to scale.

- **Services you deliver in person:** Cleaning, tutoring, pet care, moving help, handyman work, event help or personal services. Demand is local, so start where you live and ask for referrals. Check licensing and insurance first.
- **Part-time and seasonal jobs:** Retail, hospitality, warehouse and seasonal roles offer predictable hours and no startup cost. They carry the least business risk but usually the least flexibility.
- **Selling goods locally:** Markets, consignment, resale or handmade goods. You carry inventory and handling costs, so test with a small quantity and track your real costs, including your time.
- **Gig work:** Delivery or driving platforms. Platform rules, vehicle costs, insurance and local licensing vary, and platform income can change. Work out your costs per hour before you commit.

## Online options

Online work can be done from home and is not limited to your local area. It still takes time to build, and income is never automatic.

- **Freelance services:** Writing, design, administration, data work or technical support, offered to a defined type of client.
- **Digital products and teaching:** Templates, guides or short courses that solve a clear problem. See our guide to [selling digital products](/blog/how-to-sell-digital-products/).
- **Affiliate marketing:** Earning a commission when a qualifying customer action happens through your recommendation. Read the [beginner guide to affiliate marketing in Canada](/blog/affiliate-marketing-canada/) and the [comparison of affiliate programs](/blog/best-affiliate-programs-canada/).
- **Remote work and tasks:** Customer support, transcription, data labelling and similar remote tasks, including [AI training work](/blog/ai-training-jobs-guide/). Be careful with any offer that asks you to pay to get started or promises guaranteed earnings; see [how to spot job scams](/blog/ai-training-job-scams/).
- **Online selling and dropshipping:** Selling through your own store or marketplaces without holding stock. Read our [dropshipping guide for beginners](/blog/dropshipping-beginners-guide/).

## A simple way to choose

Score each idea you are considering on five points. Write the answers down instead of relying on a feeling.

1. **Permission:** Can you legally do it under your status and local rules?
2. **Startup cost:** What must you spend before your first customer?
3. **Time to first result:** Days, weeks or months?
4. **Scalability:** Does income stop when you stop working, or can it grow beyond your hours?
5. **Fit:** Does it use a skill you already have and a customer you understand?

Offline services usually score well on speed and cost but low on scale. Online products score the opposite way. Many people combine one quick-start option with one slower project that builds something lasting.

## Test one idea for 30 days

Pick a single idea. Describe the customer and the problem you solve, set a small first offer, and tell ten relevant people. Track hours, costs and responses for 30 days. At the end, decide whether to continue, adjust or stop. A cheap test that shows an idea does not work is a good result.

## Money, taxes and records

Keep a record of every payment and expense from the first dollar. The [Canada Revenue Agency explains the tax obligations of people who earn through digital platforms](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/programs/about-canada-revenue-agency-cra/compliance/platform-economy/understanding-tax-obligations.html), and other self-employment income is generally reportable too. Depending on your sales, GST/HST registration may apply; check the CRA's [registration guidance](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html) or ask a qualified tax professional.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the best side hustle in Canada?

There is no single best option. The best fit is one you are legally allowed to do, can afford to start, and can deliver with skills you already have. Compare options using permission, cost, speed, scalability and fit.

### Can I have a side hustle on a work permit?

It depends on the conditions on your permit. Some permits limit the type of work or the employer. Check your own documents with IRCC guidance or a qualified adviser before you start earning.

### Do I have to report side hustle income in Canada?

Self-employment and platform income is generally reportable for tax purposes. Keep records and check the CRA's guidance or ask a tax professional about your situation.

### How much can I earn?

It varies widely and no outcome is guaranteed. Track your own hours, costs and results over a small test before you decide whether to continue.
